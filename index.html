Exploratory Analysis of HRV Recovery Following Teacher Burnout

WHOOP Physiological Data | October 2025 – May 2026

Executive Summary

Following severe teacher burnout in September 2025, I began tracking physiological recovery using WHOOP wearable data. This project explores how Heart Rate Variability (HRV) changed during recovery.

Monthly mean HRV increased from 28.6 ms in October 2025 to 39.0 ms in April 2026 before declining slightly to 36.4 ms in May 2026.

Research Question

How did Heart Rate Variability (HRV) change during recovery from teacher burnout between October 2025 and May 2026?

Why HRV?

HRV was selected because it is one of the most widely used physiological indicators of autonomic nervous system recovery and resilience.

Because burnout is fundamentally linked to chronic stress and nervous system dysregulation, HRV provides a meaningful physiological signal through which recovery can be explored.

HRV was also preferred over WHOOP's proprietary recovery scores because it is a direct physiological measurement rather than a platform-generated metric.

Methodology

  • Data Source: WHOOP wearable device
  • Study Period: October 2025 – May 2026
  • Observations: 231 physiological records
  • Metric: Heart Rate Variability (HRV)
  • Daily HRV values grouped by month
  • Monthly means calculated to highlight long-term trends rather than daily noise

Tools Used

  • Python
  • Pandas
  • Google Colab
  • HTML
  • CSS
  • Chart.js
  • GitHub

Analytical Workflow

  1. Export physiological data from WHOOP.
  2. Import the dataset into Python using Pandas.
  3. Inspect and validate the data structure.
  4. Group daily HRV observations by calendar month.
  5. Calculate monthly mean HRV values programmatically.
  6. Visualise results using HTML, CSS and Chart.js.
  7. Interpret findings in the context of burnout recovery and major life events.
  8. Publish the analysis as a GitHub portfolio project.

Interpretation

The overall trend suggests progressive physiological recovery following severe teacher burnout. While several life events occurred during the study period, the dominant pattern is a steady improvement in HRV from October through April.

The temporary reduction observed in May coincided with dental recovery and the stress of an active job search.

Conclusion

This exploratory analysis suggests meaningful improvement in autonomic recovery capacity between October 2025 and May 2026. The project demonstrates how wearable physiological data can be used to investigate long-term recovery following burnout.
